The path

Chemistry → Pharmacometrics → AI

The through-line of my career is data. I started by generating it — one mass spectrum at a time — then learned to model the systems behind it, and now I teach machines to learn from it.

Each stage added a layer: analytical rigor from chemistry, kinetics and mechanistic thinking from research, modeling discipline from pharmacometrics, and clinical context from years of drug-development work. Machine learning didn't replace any of it — it's the layer that connects them all.

My goal: data science and AI/ML for healthcare and clinical development, where that whole stack matters.

PhD · Analytical Chemistry

University of Delaware

Studied reaction mechanisms and kinetics in aerosols using mass spectrometry — designing experiments to determine whether a reaction occurred, how fast, and by what pathway. Chemical kinetics turned out to be the same mathematics as pharmacokinetics (PK) — rate constants and differential equations — and processing large volumes of mass-spectrometry data built the analytical foundation everything since has stood on.

5+ years · CRO

Senior Research Scientist, QPS Holdings

Regulated bioanalysis in a GxP environment, noncompartmental analysis, and pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling across preclinical and clinical studies, supporting regulatory submissions including IND and NDA — where every number supports a real drug-development decision.

MS · Pharmacometrics · part-time alongside full-time work · expected 2027

University of Maryland, Baltimore

Formal training in population PK/PD — nonlinear mixed-effects modeling, what data science calls hierarchical models — and clinical trial analysis: covariate analysis, model diagnostics and selection, clinical trial simulation to support study design and dose selection, and exposure–response analysis for efficacy and safety, working hands-on with R, Julia, and NONMEM. It also grounds me in the statistics behind it all, from regression to inference. Pursued part-time alongside my full-time role at QPS — the same way I'm approaching machine learning. This is where modeling meets the clinic.

AI Agent for Pharmacometric Model Selection

Completed

An agentic workflow (Claude Code + R/mrgsolve) that runs a four-task structural model selection loop on ADaM-format PK data — fitting candidate models, comparing AIC/BIC/OFV, and pausing at a human-review checkpoint before sign-off. All three criteria converged on the two-compartment model. Along the way I documented six real engineering issues, from Windows subprocess PATH resolution to a sign-off file race condition.

Methodology adapted from Context Engineering for AI-Assisted Pharmacometrics, CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology (2026), doi 10.1002/psp4.70317.
